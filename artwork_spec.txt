The core insight
This product is not a spell checker. Spell checking is
one small module. The product is a cross-document
consistency engine that holds the job spec, the
approved artwork, the separations, and the printed
sample in one place and compares them against each
other.

F. Header consistency across pages
Job code, date, and Col: count must be identical on
every separation page and on the composite. A
mismatch means pages from two different revisions
have been mixed — a silent and expensive failure.
G. Version lock
Hash the client-approved file at approval time. Every
later comparison runs against that hash. If the vendor
worked from a different file, the hash mismatch
surfaces it immediately. This alone prevents the
single most expensive error class in the industry:
correct-looking plates made from the wrong
revision.
H. Ups / imposition count
Composite shows 4-up (2×2). Verify each separation
carries the same repeat count and positions.

4.3 Rule-based flags (machine asks, human
answers)
Same text appearing on two plates — intentional
overprint/trap, or duplication error?
Mandatory declaration on a single spot plate
Instruction present in the approval email/sheet
with no corresponding plate (varnish)
Registration mark presence and consistency
across all pages
Element position drift between plates beyond
tolerance
4.4 Out of scope for automation (human
judgement)
Trap and overprint intent
Colour choice, shade approval, ink sequence
Aesthetic decisions
The report must state these are not checked, so no
one assumes they were.
5. STEP 3 — Press-Side Check
Context constraint that drives the design: the
machine is stopped, an operator and QC are waiting,
and the whole check must complete in under ~60


--- Page 14 ---
seconds. A flatbed scanner at the press is not
practical. Mobile camera is a core requirement, not
an optional phase.
5.1 Why this is technically easier than
general OCR
The system already knows what the text should be.
This is constrained verification, not open-ended
reading. Expected text acts as a dictionary and
expected layout gives positional priors — accuracy is
far higher than general-purpose OCR on the same
image.
5.2 Scope reduction after Step 2
If Step 2 passed, content is already verified. Step 3
only needs to answer: are all plates mounted and
printing?
On the test job that means checking:
Gold elements present (top border, 100ml band,
"Kalonji Oil" underline)
Green "DAILY PHARMA" present
Varnish window applied
All four CMYK images present
Registration marks aligned
This is spot-check, not full-text OCR — which is
what makes the 60-second budget achievable.


--- Page 15 ---
5.3 Capture design
Corner alignment guides on screen — user
frames the label. Enables
homography/perspective correction so the
captured image is warped into the approved
artwork's geometry
Burst capture — take 3–5 frames, auto-select
sharpest. Invisible to the user
Pre-loaded job — the approved PDF must be
selected during machine setup, before the press
stops. At the press: photo only
Works on a stopped press with a sample pulled
onto the inspection table (flat, reasonable light) —
not on a moving web
5.4 Output must never be PASS/FAIL
3 areas need your eyes:
  ⚠  Gold plate — "Kalonji Oil" underline 
not detected
  ⚠  MRP zone — low confidence, check 
manually
  ⚠  Green plate — "DAILY PHARMA" faint
  47 other elements matched  ✓
Reasoning: this converts a 200-word manual read
into looking at 3 specific places. It keeps the human as


--- Page 16 ---
the decision-maker, which is both operationally
correct and the right liability posture (§10).
5.5 Offline requirement
Press floors have poor connectivity. Step 3 must work
fully offline with the job pre-downloaded, syncing
results when connection returns.
6. Additional Modules (beyond the
three steps)
These are what turn a tool into a product a printer will
pay monthly for.
6.1 Digital sign-off & audit trail — HIGH
PRIORITY
Replaces the blank paper sign-off boxes. Each stage
records who approved, what file version, timestamp,
device. Immutable log.
Why this matters commercially: it converts the
vendor's liability disclaimer from a threat into a
defence. When a dispute happens, the printer has a
record.
6.2 Revision management


--- Page 17 ---
Rev 01, 02, 03… with diff between revisions. Answers
"what changed since last time?" — currently answered
by memory.
6.3 Customer approval portal
Client receives a link, views artwork, approves or
comments. Approval is captured with hash +
timestamp. Removes the email chain entirely.
6.4 Job library & re-order verification
Repeat orders are common. Store every approved job.
On re-order, diff the new file against the last approved
version — usually only MRP or batch format changed.
Turns a full review into a 10-second diff.
6.5 Reports
Exportable PDF per job: what was checked, results,
who signed off, file hashes. Attach to the job bag.
Send to client. Use in disputes.
6.6 Braille inspection (Phase 3)
Required for pharma exports to the EU. Detection, dot
spacing, decoded value. Only relevant if pharma
export customers are targeted.
6.7 Font size verification


--- Page 18 ---
Legal Metrology prescribes minimum heights for
specified declarations. Measure actual rendered
height in the PDF against the rule pack threshold.
6.8 Colour / plate ink sequence record
Not colour approval (needs spectrophotometer), but
recording the declared sequence and flagging
deviation from the job sheet.
6.9 Ink coverage estimate (nice-to-have)
Per-plate coverage percentage from separations.
Useful for costing — and links naturally to the
calculator tools discussed separately.
6.10 Multi-language declaration check
Indian labels often carry declarations in multiple
languages. Verify each required language block is
present per the rule pack.
7. Compliance Rule Packs — How
Norms Actually Work
This is the most important architectural decision in
the product.
7.1 The principle


--- Page 19 ---
The application does not know any law. It knows
how to apply a rule pack.
Rules are never hardcoded. They are versioned JSON
documents fetched from the server. When a
regulation changes, a new rule pack is published —
every client gets it immediately, with no app update,
no Play Store release, no per-customer deployment.

7.4 How a pack is selected for a job
During job setup: product category + jurisdiction (+
state, where relevant). The system loads the matching
pack and shows which pack version was used in the
report.
7.5 Three honest limits — must be visible in
the product
1. The app is not a compliance authority. It runs a
checklist someone wrote. Report wording: "11 of 12
checklist items found. 1 not detected." Never
"COMPLIANT ✓".


--- Page 22 ---
2. Rule packs need a maintainer. Initially you. As
the pack library grows this becomes real ongoing
work — and eventually a reason to charge more,
or to partner with a compliance consultant.
3. Every pack carries its own provenance —
source reference, last-verified date, verifier. Visible
to the user. If a pack is stale, say so.
7.6 Customer-specific packs
Beyond regulation: a brand's own rules ("logo clear
space ≥ 5mm", "always include #TAGLINE", "MRP font
never below 8pt"). Same engine, customer-scoped
pack. This is a strong upsell and a retention lock.
8. Barcode & QR Verification
Two entirely different problems. Keep them separate in
the product.
8.1 In artwork (Step 1 / Step 2) — fully
solvable
Extract the barcode from the PDF (vector or
embedded raster), then:
Decode the symbol
Checksum validation — EAN-13/UPC check digit
must be correct. A wrong check digit means the


--- Page 23 ---
barcode will not scan, and this is entirely
deterministic
GS1 structure parsing — GTIN, and Application
Identifiers for batch (10), expiry (17), serial (21)
where present
Value match — decoded GTIN vs the value
recorded in the job spec. Catches the case where
artwork carries a previous product's barcode
Quiet zone — required clear space present
X-dimension / magnification — within the
standard's permitted range for the symbology
Bar Width Reduction — flexo requires BWR
compensation. Record what the job spec declares
and whether it was applied
Colour combination — bars must be dark on light
with adequate contrast. Flag red/light bars, or bars
printed on a spot colour with poor contrast
QR — decode, verify payload matches spec,
check error-correction level and module size
8.2 On printed sample (Step 3) — partial
only, be honest
Decode / no-decode — a phone camera can
reliably tell you whether the printed barcode scans
at all. This is genuinely useful and catches the
worst failure


--- Page 24 ---
Formal ISO/IEC 15416 grading (A–F) — not
possible with a phone camera. Grading requires
a calibrated verifier with controlled illumination and
aperture
Product rule: never display a grade from a phone
capture. Show "scanned successfully" or "failed to
scan", and state that formal grading requires a verifier.
Claiming a grade you cannot measure destroys
credibility with exactly the customers who know the
difference.

9.2 Why processing cannot live in Flutter or
Cloud Functions
PDF text extraction with position data, page rendering,
image diffing, homography, and barcode decoding
need mature native libraries. Cloud Functions are too
constrained on memory, execution time, and binary
dependencies for multi-page separation PDFs.
Run a Python service on Cloud Run:
PyMuPDF — text with coordinates, font metrics,
page rendering
pdfplumber — table and layout structure
OpenCV — image diff, alignment, homography

Pillow , NumPy — image handling
OCR: PaddleOCR or Tesseract (constrained
mode with expected-text dictionary)
Flutter handles UI, capture, and job workflow. All heavy
work is an API call.

10. Liability Positioning
The vendor already prints "Customers are advised to
check plates carefully before printing." This product


--- Page 28 ---
must not absorb that liability.
Rules baked into the product:
1. Never output "APPROVED", "PASS", or
"COMPLIANT". Output findings and counts.
2. Every report states what was not checked (colour,
trap intent, aesthetics).
3. Every report names the human who signed off.
4. Rule pack version and last-verified date appear on
every compliance report.
5. Terms of service state clearly: decision-support
tool, not a guarantee.
Framed correctly, this is a selling point, not a
weakness: the product gives the printer a
documented, timestamped record of diligence —
which is exactly what they currently lack when a
dispute arises.

12. Build Order & Milestones
Phase 0 — Validation (2–3 weeks)


--- Page 30 ---
Script-only, no UI. Run Step 2 checks over the last 20
jobs at PGPL.
Reprocess the Kalonji job — the varnish gap must
be caught. This is regression test #1
Count how many other real errors surface
Decision gate: if 20 historical jobs surface fewer
than 2–3 genuine issues, reconsider scope before
building further
Phase 1 — Step 2, web only (6–8 weeks)
Job spec entry, three-file upload, all deterministic
checks, findings report, PDF export.
Success: PGPL uses it on every job for one month
without falling back to manual.
Phase 2 — Step 1 + sign-off (6–8 weeks)
Spell check, variable-field marking, first two rule packs
(Legal Metrology, FSSAI), barcode artwork checks,
digital sign-off, audit trail, revision management.
Success: 3 external printers paying.
Phase 3 — Step 3, Flutter APK (8–10 weeks)
Camera capture with alignment guides, offline job
cache, spot-check comparison, sync.
Success: used at the press on live jobs, under 60
seconds per check.


--- Page 31 ---
Phase 4 — Scale
Customer approval portal, job library re-order diff,
more rule packs, on-premise option, integrations.
Why Step 2 first: cheapest to build (pure PDF, no
camera, no OCR), highest saving per catch (before
plates are made), and there is already a proven failure
case to test against.
